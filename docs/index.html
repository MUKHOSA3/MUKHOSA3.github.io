Conference Overview

Sankhya 2024 conference emphasizes the use of statistical and quantitative sciences in drug discovery, development, life cycle management, as well as daily operations and business services. The 13th edition of this series is a collaborative effort between Development Advanced Quantitative Sciences (AQS), India, and Global Business Solutions (GBS), India.

The conference will feature scientific talks and poster presentations on a variety of topics, including case studies, regulatory guidelines, and business needs. The presenters will comprise associates from Novartis and other pharmaceutical companies.

Your participation in this event is greatly appreciated.

The Keynote Speaker:

Dr Arijit Nag, MD (Internal Medicine), DM (Clinical Hematology), MRCP (UK); Tata Medical Center, Kolkata

Dr. Arijit Nag is a Clinical Scientist in the Department of Clinical Hematology and Cellular Therapies at Tata Medical Center, Kolkata. As a dedicated hematologist with a keen interest in Leukemia biology, he specialises in treating patients with Acute Leukemia and performing stem cell transplantation. His work is driven by the goal of improving survival outcomes for patients with Acute Myeloid Leukemia and Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia. Dr. Nag is committed to making innovative treatments more accessible and available to his patients, ensuring they receive the best possible care.

09:45 to 10:40 - Keynote Address by Dr. Arijit Nag

Title: Harnessing the power of biostatistics in cancer medicine

Speaker: Dr Arijit Nag, Dr Arijit Nag, MD (Internal Medicine), DM (Clinical Hematology), MRCP (UK); Tata Medical Center, Kolkata

Abstract: Biostatistics plays a crucial role in advancing cancer medicine by providing essential tools for designing studies, analyzing data, and interpreting results. Biostatistics is at the heart of cancer research, and we shall analyse its impact on clinical trials, epidemiological studies, and personalized medicine. By employing rigorous statistical techniques, researchers can identify risk factors, evaluate treatment efficacy, and optimize patient outcomes.

Recent advancements, such as the application of machine learning and big data analytics, have further enhanced the capacity of biostatistics to address complex challenges in oncology. One such instance is the development of predictive models that guide treatment decisions and improve prognostic accuracy. Additionally, the use of bioinformatics in conjunction with biostatistics facilitates the integration of genomic data, enabling a more nuanced understanding of tumor behavior and patient heterogeneity.

Collaborative efforts between biostatisticians and clinical researchers are essential for translating statistical findings into actionable insights. This partnership fosters the design of innovative trials, such as adaptive designs, which can respond dynamically to interim results, ultimately leading to more efficient drug development processes.

Harnessing the power of biostatistics in cancer medicine not only enhances the robustness of research findings but also paves the way for more personalised and effective therapeutic strategies. As the field continues to evolve, the synergy between biostatistics and cancer research will remain pivotal in shaping the future of oncology and improving patient care outcomes.

10:50 to 11:10 - Identifying Subgroups In Phase II Clinical Trials by Rajkumar Radhakrishnan (Novartis)

Title: Identifying Subgroups In Phase II Clinical Trials

Author(s): Rajkumar Radhakrishnan

Abstract: Subgroup analyses remain a key element to identify if any particular group of interest differs from the overall population in a clinical trial setting. There exists quite a bit of uncertainty and risk around these findings, if any while communicating to the decision-makers. Model averaging is one such technique which provides a conservative approach to mitigate the problem of chance findings due to random high/bias. This approach will be illustrated with a clinical trial example with 9 subgroups. The results generated from the R-shiny application (go/subtee) will be presented and discussed briefly to illustrate the shrinkage effect in both Bayesian and frequentist setting compared to unadjusted inference and enabling robust decisions.

Conclusions: This presentation is about the robust methodology that takes risk/uncertainties into account analytically and alleviate any bias to avoid false positive signals.

Keywords: Bayesian inference , model averaging , shrinkage

11:10 to 11:30 - Brief Description Of The Key Methodological Aspects Of A Vaccine Trial With An Emphasis On A Covid-19 Vaccine Study by Pritam Gupta (BMS)

Title: Brief Description Of The Key Methodological Aspects Of A Vaccine Trial With An Emphasis On A Covid-19 Vaccine Study

Author(s): Pritam Gupta

Abstract: Vaccines are complex biological products which are administered to healthy individuals. Safety is therefore paramount; vaccine development often entails large, time-consuming, and resource-intensive studies to detect rare safety issues and to establish vaccine efficacy. Before a vaccine is licensed and brought to the market, it undergoes a long and rigorous process of research, followed by many years of clinical testing. This talk will focus on introducing some of the key concepts and terminologies related to vaccine trials, discussing on common statistical methodologies related to analyzing trial data followed by the detailed description of the seamless development paradigm used to develop a messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA) vaccine for Covid-19. The talk will include key statistical aspects and regulatory challenges of the designs with an emphasis on the Bayesian framework implemented, which enabled us to obtain efficient designs using decision criteria based on the probability of benefit or harm. Publicly disclosed results will be summarized and discussed. Finally, the talk will conclude by describing my role as a blinded safety statistician involved with quality checks and review of multiple safety outputs on a real time basis.

Conclusions: Seamless plan was efficient to make the vaccine available in record time. Bayesian framework provided a straightforward way to design and implement a clinically meaningful interim analysis plan. Safety and efficacy profile were favorable and supported European Union approval (EUA) and conditional approvals around the world. Once in a lifetime opportunity to support such a high priority impactful trial.

Keywords: mRNA , Covid-19 , Bayesian and vaccine efficacy

11:30 to 11:50 - Analysis Of Patient Reported Outcome Measures (PROMs) – Estimation Of Meaningful Within-Patient Change by Sebastian Thomas (Novo Nordisk)

Title: Analysis Of Patient Reported Outcome Measures (PROMs) – Estimation Of Meaningful Within-Patient Change

Author(s): Sebastian Thomas

Abstract: The notion that magnitude of an effect that prompts statistical significance may not be clinically relevant has been a long-standing topic of discussion for many years. The Food and Drug Application (FDA) acknowledges the need for a patient-centric drug development approach to design and implement studies to capture the patient’s voice in a robust manner.

The sponsors identify threshold(s) for the change in COA (clinical outcome assessment) that constitute a meaningful within-patient change (MWPC) for the target participant population, by anchor-based and distribution-based approaches. The idea is to relate the target patient-reported outcome (PRO) instrument to an independent measure – the anchor, which is relevant to patient well-being. The empirical cumulative distribution function (eCDF) and probability density function (PDF) curves is used to access whether the treatment effect occurs in the range that is considered clinically meaningful by the patients.

The paper primarily focuses on the application of these methods to establish the MWPC for physical functioning in subjects with knee osteoarthritis considering a simulated dataset. The research also outlines my proposal of a best- and worst- case scenario for handling missingness in PRO that lacks construct validity and internal consistency using ideas from Real analysis which is first of its kind till date.

Conclusions: There was a stronger response correlation between the anchor measure and target COA. The MWPC thresholds are derived for the physical functioning subscale based on 1-category improvement on the related PGI-S (Patient Global Impression of Severity, primary anchor) and PGI-C (Patient Global Impression of Change, supportive anchor). The clinical relevance of the treatment effect is assessed based on the proportion of participants in each treatment group who have experienced the clinically relevant improvements (responders) in the target COA. The innovative methods described in the paper are employed by Novo Nordisk for the first time in-house for one of the ongoing studies.

Regarding the best- and worst- case scenario, the overall participant perception can be assessed based on the amount of deviation of the imputed score by assuming Missingness at Random (MAR) to the worst and best totals. The proposal echoes with the FDA’s argument of clearly differentiating MWPC from between-group difference.

The presentation also highlights the importance for a close collaboration among different stakeholders in interpreting the thresholds and to evaluate its implications for optimising future studies in providing clinically meaningful benefit to patients.

Keywords: Patient-reported outcomes , Meaningful within-patient change , Anchor-based approach and Distribution-based approach

11:50 to 12:10 - A Feasibility Study To Assess The Adequacy Of Historical Clinical Trial Data Using AmBisome® To Inform The Development Of An Innovative Oral Treatment (LXE408) For Primary Visceral Leishmaniasis (VL) by Rashidkhan Pathan (Novartis)

Title: A Feasibility Study To Assess The Adequacy Of Historical Clinical Trial Data Using AmBisome® To Inform The Development Of An Innovative Oral Treatment (LXE408) For Primary Visceral Leishmaniasis (VL)

Author(s): Rashidkhan Pathan, Gerhild Angyalosi, Kirsten Carter, Rashidkhan Pathan and Rima Izem

Abstract: Novartis has partnered with DNDi (Drugs for Neglected Disease initiative) to develop LXE408, a new oral treatment for visceral leishmaniasis (VL). VL is a neglected tropical disease prevalent in areas with limited healthcare access. The program is currently in Phase II, with a study ongoing in India to determine the safety and effectiveness of the treatment. A Phase III confirmatory non-inferiority study comparing LXE408 to the current standard is planned. However, the required sample size may be challenging to recruit in a reasonable timeframe, due to reduction of VL cases in recent years resulting from a successful VL elimination program. To address this, the team plans to incorporate external control data from a previous study, which will enhance statistical power and accelerate evidence generation. By combining individual patient data from this external control study with the Phase III trial data, the non-inferiority of the new treatment can be determined more precisely. Subject to agreement with regulatory authorities, the objective is to use existing (historical) data with the standard of care to accelerate the development of LXE408 and hence offering a convenient oral alternative to the current standard of care, which is an infusion that requires administration in specialized centers. Research objectives: The research objectives include the following: - Identify subject-level data with granular patient characteristics information - Explore cohort characteristics and heterogeneity - Evaluate feasibility of including all or a subset of the data as external controls to complement internal control in the study - If feasible, develop methods and codes to use the data

Methods: Outcome measure: Primary Endpoint is the definitive cure at Day 180. It is defined as the initial cure at Day 28, no requirement for rescue treatment throughout the study, no death due to any reason and absence of any clinical and laboratory parameters of VL at Day 180 (i.e. no relapse). Any patient who receives rescue treatment for VL or treatment for post-kala-azar dermal leishmaniasis (PKDL) during the study will be considered a treatment failure at Day 180. Our methodology will proceed in two stages, an exploratory stage to evaluate feasibility of using the external database, and if feasible an analytical stage that will leverage existing data with completed clinical trial data. A target trial framework will be applied to select the patients from the external control to limit the selection bias. To explore baseline characteristics of the external control and compare those to the current study. Report summary statistics of each baseline characteristic and compare to the same characteristic in ongoing study using t-test for continuous characteristic, and chi-square test for categorical characteristic. Balance in each characteristic will also be explored using standardized mean differences (SMD). To explore balance in the covariates across all baseline characteristic, a propensity score model using a logistic regression including all the potential confounders will be fitted. Additionally, Bayesian borrowing (BB) methods such as Power prior methods will be considered to augment an external control arm using historical clinical trial data. If feasible with limited sources of bias, the data from the subset of patients will be augmented with the internal control of a present and explore changes in primary and secondary outcome by prognostic factor using univariate or multivariable logistic regression.

Conclusions: Next steps: following feasibility and method development, the team will engage with regulatory authorities to confirm adequacy of the approach and acceptance for phase III study.

Keywords: External control , Bayesian bowrrowing , real world evidence

13:30 to 13:50 - The Art Of Dynamic Functions: Enhancing R’s Capabilities With Metaprogramming by Aquib Ansari (Novartis)

Title: The Art Of Dynamic Functions: Enhancing R’s Capabilities With Metaprogramming

Author(s): Aquib Ansari

Abstract: The advancement of open-source technologies in clinical domains has led to a significant increase in the generation of R code by programmers. However, this has also raised the question of whether it is necessary to generate code repeatedly for the same tasks. One solution to address this issue is the creation of functions. However, evaluating column names as arguments in nested functions using base R techniques can be challenging and often results in suboptimal outputs.

This abstract focuses on addressing these challenges by harnessing metaprogramming techniques to enhance the dynamic nature of functions in R. By using metaprogramming, column expressions can be effectively handled as parameters within functions. This approach simplifies the manipulation of variables passed as arguments in user-defined functions, which can be challenging when relying on basic R operators such as $ and [[]]. This can be overcome by utilizing functions that embrace metaprogramming principles, such as eval(), expr(), parse(), sym(), and syms().

Conclusions: By incorporating metaprogramming, the handling of column expressions within functions is streamlined, resulting in improved ease of use and efficiency. This concept enhances the utility and flexibility of R programmers, seamlessly integrating with packages like tidyverse. By leveraging metaprogramming techniques, R programmers can create functions that are more dynamic and adaptable.

Keywords: Metaprogramming , Functions , R

13:50 to 14:10 - Harnessing Casual Inference Methodologies In Real-World Data For Pharmaceutical Insights by Venugopala Rao Manneni (Novartis)

Title: Harnessing Casual Inference Methodologies In Real-World Data For Pharmaceutical Insights

Author(s): Venugopala Rao Manneni, Revathy R and Saurabh Kumar

Abstract: Background: Real-world data (RWD) presents valuable prospects for research within pharmaceutical industry. However, correlation does not imply causation. Establishing true causal associations using RWD is beset with challenges due to potential biases inherent within the data. To enhance the credibility of RWD evidence and ensure its comparability to clinical trials, utilization of causal inference methodologies is vital.

Objective: To showcase effectiveness of casual inference methodologies in mitigating confounding biases, and accurately identifying treatment effects while generating RWD evidence.

Proposed Methodology: We will explore a RWD dataset to demonstrate various causal inference methodologies including a) propensity score matching for confounder mitigation, b) uplift modelling to predict treatment effects, c) instrumental variables to tackle unmeasured confounders, and d) difference-in-differences for temporal outcome comparisons. We will also conduct a comparison, contrasting the outcomes of analysis that utilize these methodologies with those that do not.

Expected outcome: Our analysis utilizing these methodologies would yield more precise treatment effect estimates. By comparing analysis with/without casual inference methodologies, we will be able to underscore the indispensability of these techniques. The outcome would demonstrate that the quality of RWD evidence could be comparable to clinical trials. This would further improve the utility of RWD evidence in decision making.

Conclusions: Conclusion: In conclusion, the RWD research provides exceptional prospects for observational studies in the pharmaceutical industry, aiding in drug development, post-market surveillance, and decision making. This study will confirm that the utilization of casual inference methodologies is imperative for identifying true casual associations. By employing these advanced artificial intelligence techniques, the researchers can approximate the reliability of RWD evidence to randomized control trials, that could shape informed decisions in the pharmaceutical industry.

Keywords: casual inference methodologies , real-world data , Confounder

14:10 to 14:30 - Application Of Causal Inference And Estimand Framework In Evaluating The Effectiveness From An Observational Real-World Evidence (RWE) Study by Sanchayita Sadhu (GSK)

Title: Application Of Causal Inference And Estimand Framework In Evaluating The Effectiveness From An Observational Real-World Evidence (RWE) Study

Author(s): Sanchayita Sadhu

Abstract: Baseline covariates balancing is a crucial step in causal inference for observational studies. The goal is to ensure that the treatment and control groups are similar in terms of observed covariates before the treatment effect is estimated. The aim is to reduce bias in the estimated treatment effect that could be caused by confounding variables. Along with that, the Estimand framework helps define the specific causal question of interest and guide the statistical analysis. It provides a clear definition of the treatment effect to be estimated, considering the possible interferences like post-baseline events. This presentation will explore the application of causal inference and Estimand framework in comparing the real-world effectiveness of Treatment a over Treatment B in terms of overall asthma exacerbation among patients with asthma in general practice cohort. This presentation discuss the use of propensity score based methods like propensity score matching (PSM) and inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW), for baseline covariates balancing in observational studies. It also outlines the challanges associated with this methods and introduces an alternative approach, Propensity Score with Overlap Weights (PSOW). This presentation also explores the best possible Estimand framework for the given scenario and describes its causal expressions.

Conclusions: This presentation concludes by emphasizing the importance of causal inference and Estimand strategies in observational studies and the need for further reasearch on suitable stabilization methods for IPTW or the implementation of PSOW.

Keywords: Causal inference , Estimand framework , Propensity score weighting methods and Propensity Score with Overlap Weights

14:30 to 14:50 - Network Analysis Of Omics Data: A Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) Approach For Pharmaceutical Insights by Apoorva Sood (Novo Nordisk)

Title: Network Analysis Of Omics Data: A Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) Approach For Pharmaceutical Insights

Author(s): Apoorva Sood

Abstract: Omics studies includes biological information derived from various “-omics” technologies such as genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics. Omics data comprises of millions of data points being processed at once and it becomes crucial to highlight the patterns amongst them. It paves the way for improved diagnostics, targeted therapies, and personalized medicine. The Weighted Gene Co-expression Network Analysis (WGCNA) package in R provides a robust framework for unraveling complex gene relationships. This study showcases the application of WGCNA to construct a co-expression network, employing a soft thresholding power selection to capture meaningful connections. Utilizing graph theory and community detection algorithms, we further characterized these modules, unveiling key gene interactions and functional relationships. For the visualization purpose the visNetwork package in R is used that helps to interactively explore protein or gene modules along with their interconnections.

Conclusions: The WGCNA allows us to identify gene modules and explore relationships among genes, shedding light on complex molecular interactions and biological pathways. By identifying gene modules associated with specific diseases or drug responses, discovery of potential biomarkers can be supported. The application of this package in the medical field holds great potential for advancing precision medicine, drug discovery, and the understanding of complex diseases.

Keywords: Omics , Network Analysis , Biomarkers and Gene Modules.

15:30 to 15:50 - The Integral Role Of Statisticians In Clinical Development Planning: A Case Study by Rupam Ranjan Pal (GSK)

Title: The Integral Role Of Statisticians In Clinical Development Planning: A Case Study

Author(s): Rupam Ranjan Pal

Abstract: This presentation delves into the pivotal role of a statistician in the formulation of a Clinical Development Plan (CDP) for a new molecule. The journey of a molecule from discovery to a therapeutic drug involves a strategic vision, encapsulated in a medicine profile. This profile outlines the potential disease areas or indications for which the molecule could be developed into a medicine, along with the varying degrees of anticipated therapeutic benefits for each indication.

The CDP is a comprehensive strategy devised by the sponsor to realize this vision. It employs a robust decision-making framework that enhances the likelihood of achieving the desired outcomes. The process often necessitates the creation of multiple CDPs, each with its unique advantages, drawbacks, estimated development costs, and future sales predictions. These plans are then presented to the upper management for informed decision-making regarding future investments.

Statisticians play a crucial role in this process. Their responsibilities extend from designing clinical trials for all phases and indications, determining the Probability of Success (PoS) for each trial, to calculating the overall PoS for the molecule in each indication/disease for every development plan. Their contributions significantly influence the governance body’s decisions on future investments.

This presentation will illustrate an example of a CDP, emphasizing the statistician’s contribution and its impact on investment decisions.

Conclusions: Attendees will gain insights into the integral role of statisticians in clinical development planning and decision-making processes.

Keywords: Clinical Development Plan , Medicine Vision , Probability of Success

15:50 to 16:10 - Implementation Of Bayesian Optimal Interval (BOIN) Design For Dose Finding Radioligand Therapy (RLT) Study Of Lutathera In Newly Diagnosed Glioblastoma And In Recurrent Glioblastoma by Bijoy Kumar Dey (Novartis)

Title: Implementation Of Bayesian Optimal Interval (BOIN) Design For Dose Finding Radioligand Therapy (RLT) Study Of Lutathera In Newly Diagnosed Glioblastoma And In Recurrent Glioblastoma

Author(s): Bijoy Kumar Dey, Ravi Loka and Arnaud Demange

Abstract: Radioligand therapy (RLT) is a medical treatment that uses radioactive substances (radioligands) to deliver radiation directly to targeted cells or tissues, such as cancer cells. It is a form of targeted therapy that combines the properties of radiation therapy and targeted molecular therapies. A phase Ib study for the treatment of subjects with newly diagnosed Glioblastoma in combination with radiotherapy and Temozolomide and in Recurrent Glioblastoma as single agent, is a dose finding study using Bayesian Optimal Interval (BOIN) Design. The primary objective of the study is to determine the recommended dose of Lutathera in 2 different groups, newly diagnosed Glioblastoma in combination with radiotherapy and Temozolomide and Recurrent Glioblastoma as single agent. Despite the therapeutic options currently available, prognosis of glioblastoma is dismal. Given the significant unmet medical need in glioblastoma, it is evident that new therapeutic approaches are needed. Our objective is to discuss about BOIN Design, how this design is implemented in this study and how maximum Tolerated Dose (MTD) is estimated. We also plan to discuss how RLT works on the disease area. We demonstrate the operating characteristics of the design with motivating simulated examples. The design is implemented with the companion R package ‘BOIN’. We will discuss the salient features of different designs such as BOIN or Bayesian Logistic Regression Model (BLRM).

Conclusions: The benefit of this presentation is to understand about Bayesian Optimum Interval Design, how this design is implemented in clinical study and how this design helps us to estimate Maximum Tolerated Dose. By this presentation, also everyone will also gain some information about RLT.

Keywords: BOIN Design , Radioligand Therapy , Glioblastoma
